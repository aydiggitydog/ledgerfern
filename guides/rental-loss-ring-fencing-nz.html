Rental property losses and ring-fencing in NZ, explained (2026)

Two rules decide how much tax you pay on a NZ residential rental in 2026: how much mortgage interest you can deduct, and what happens to a loss. One of them changed recently, and the other didn't.

Interest: back to 100% from 1 April 2025

From 2021, interest on most residential rentals was gradually made non-deductible. That has been reversed. From 1 April 2025, interest is fully deductible again (it was 80% in the 2024–25 year). Inland Revenue confirms this on its page about changes to the interest limitation rules.

Ring-fencing: unchanged

Since the 2019–20 tax year, residential rental losses have been "ring-fenced". That means a rental loss can't reduce your tax on salary, wages or business income. Instead, the loss carries forward and is used against rental profits in future years. Restoring interest deductibility didn't change this. In fact, a bigger interest deduction makes a loss more likely for highly geared properties.

Worked example

A couple owns two rentals and has $3,200 of ring-fenced losses from last year.

Property 1Property 2Total
Rent$5,200$3,900$9,100
Interest (100%)$1,480$1,320$2,800
Other deductible costs$3,800$2,985$6,785
Net−$80−$405−$485

The portfolio made a $485 loss. They can't use it against their wages, so it's added to the losses carried forward: $3,200 + $485 = $3,685 carried into next year. Their taxable rental income this year is $0.

If next year the portfolio makes a $4,915 profit instead, the $3,685 is used first, and only $1,230 is taxable.

What isn't deductible

  • Loan principal repayments. Only the interest is deductible.
  • Capital improvements: adding something new or upgrading, such as installing a heat pump where there wasn't one, an extension, or a new kitchen. Repairing what's already there is deductible. Improving it generally isn't.
  • Private use, such as weeks when you stay at the property yourself.

Portfolio or property by property?

By default, ring-fencing works across your whole residential portfolio, so a profit on one property can absorb a loss on another. You can elect to apply it property by property, which has consequences when you sell. A tax agent can tell you whether that suits you.

Also worth knowing

  • Residential rent is GST-exempt, so you don't charge GST and you can't claim GST on costs.
  • Rental income and expenses go on the IR3R schedule with your IR3.

Sources: Inland Revenue, changes to the interest limitation rules (ird.govt.nz); Simpson Grierson, "Full interest deductibility for residential rental properties returns but no change to loss ring-fencing".
