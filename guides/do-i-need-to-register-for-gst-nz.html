Do I need to register for GST in NZ? The $60,000 rule explained

GST registration is the first tax decision most new sole traders face. The rule is simple once you know there are two parts to it.

The rule

You must register for GST if you carry on a taxable activity (a business, trade or ongoing activity where you supply goods or services for payment) and either:

  • your turnover in the last 12 months was $60,000 or more, or
  • you expect your turnover in the next 12 months to be $60,000 or more.

"Turnover" means the total value of your sales, not your profit. Expenses don't reduce it.

The test is rolling, not by tax year

The 12 months can be any 12 months, not just 1 April to 31 March. A designer who invoices $4,000 a month passes $60,000 over months 1 to 15. Check your last 12 months of income regularly once you're above about $4,000 a month on average.

The forward-looking part matters too. If you sign a contract in March that will pay $6,000 a month for the next year, you're expected to exceed $60,000, so you need to register now, not in 10 months' time.

Registering below $60,000 (voluntary registration)

If you have a taxable activity, you can choose to register even if you're under the threshold. Whether it's worth it depends on who your customers are:

Registering voluntarily tends to suit you if…Staying unregistered tends to suit you if…
Your customers are mostly GST-registered businesses (they claim the GST back, so it doesn't cost them)Your customers are mostly the public (adding 15% makes you dearer, or comes out of your margin)
You have big start-up costs with GST on them (tools, equipment, a vehicle)Your costs are low or mostly GST-free
You're likely to pass $60,000 soon anywayYou want the least admin possible

What changes once you register

  • You add 15% GST to your prices (or treat your prices as GST-inclusive) and show your GST number on invoices.
  • You claim back the GST on your business purchases.
  • You file a GST return every 1, 2 or 6 months. Two-monthly is the default, and six-monthly is available if your turnover is $500,000 or less.
  • Your income tax is worked out on GST-exclusive figures.

Common questions

Does wage income count towards the $60,000? No. Only sales from your taxable activity count.

Do exported services count? Yes. Zero-rated sales are still taxable supplies, so they count towards turnover.

I passed $60,000 without noticing. What now? Register as soon as you can and talk to IRD or a tax agent about the period you missed. IRD can backdate a registration.

How to register

Register in myIR (or through a tax agent). You'll choose your filing frequency and accounting basis (most sole traders use the payments basis) when you register.

Sources: Inland Revenue, "Registering for GST" and the GST guide (IR375).
