Home office expenses for NZ sole traders: both methods, worked

If you run your business from home, part of your household costs is a business expense. IRD offers two ways to work it out. Both start with the same question: how much of your home is used for the business?

Step 1: work out your business area

Measure the space you use mainly for business, such as a dedicated office or workshop, and compare it with the total floor area of your home.

Example: a 12 m² office in a 120 m² house is 10%.

A dining table you also eat at doesn't count as a set-aside area. The space needs to be used mainly for the business.

Method 1: the floor-area (proportional) method

Add up your actual household premises costs for the year and claim the business percentage of each. Typical costs:

  • power and gas
  • house insurance
  • council rates
  • mortgage interest (interest only, not principal) or rent
  • repairs to the house in general

Phone and internet are handled separately, by how much you actually use them for business, not by floor area.

Method 2: IRD’s square metre rate

Instead of tracking power and similar running costs, you multiply your business area by a rate IRD publishes each year. Mortgage interest, rates and rent are still claimed separately by floor-area share. IRD’s formula is:

(mortgage interest + rates + rent) × business % + business m² × the square metre rate

The published rate for the 2026 income year (1 April 2025 to 31 March 2026) is $57.30 per m². IRD publishes each year’s rate after the year ends, so check ird.govt.nz for the 2026–27 figure before you file.

Same example, using the 2025–26 rate:

  • ($15,000 interest + $3,000 rates) × 10% = $1,800
  • 12 m² × $57.30 = $687.60
  • Claim: $2,487.60

Under this option you can't claim other premises costs (such as the house insurance in Method 1) on top. The rate is meant to cover them.

Which is better?

Do the sums both ways for your own figures. The square metre rate is less record-keeping, since you don't need to split every power bill. It often comes out ahead when your running costs are low. The floor-area method can be higher if your power, heating or insurance costs are large.

Records to keep

  • A floor plan or measurements showing the business area and the total area.
  • The bills you claim a share of (or, for the square metre rate, your interest, rates and rent statements).
  • A note of how you worked it out. Keep records for 7 years.

Sources: Inland Revenue, "Home office expenses" and "Square metre rate for home office calculations 2026".
