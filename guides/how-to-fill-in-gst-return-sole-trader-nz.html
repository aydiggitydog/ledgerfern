How to fill in a GST return as a sole trader in NZ (box by box)

If you're a GST-registered sole trader, you file a GST return every one, two or six months. The return in myIR has numbered boxes that follow the paper GST101A form. Once your records are in order, each box is simple. Here's what goes in each one, with a worked example.

Before you start: what you need

  • A list of every payment you received in the period (if you use the payments basis, which most sole traders do).
  • A list of every business expense you paid in the period, with the receipts or invoices (IRD calls this "taxable supply information").
  • Your period dates, which you can find in myIR.

The sales side: boxes 5 to 10

Box 5, total sales and income: everything you received for your taxable activity, including GST. Include zero-rated sales such as exported services. Leave out exempt income, such as residential rent or interest.

Box 6, zero-rated supplies: the part of Box 5 that had 0% GST, typically services supplied to overseas clients.

Box 7: Box 5 minus Box 6.

Box 8, GST on sales: Box 7 × 3 ÷ 23. (3/23 is the GST part of a GST-inclusive amount at 15%.)

Box 9, debit adjustments: usually zero for a sole trader. It's used, for example, when you take something you claimed GST on for private use.

Box 10: Box 8 plus Box 9. This is your total GST collected.

The purchases side: boxes 11 to 14

Box 11, total purchases and expenses: the GST-inclusive total of business purchases that had GST charged. Leave out:

  • bank fees, loan interest and ACC levies (no GST is charged on them)
  • purchases from suppliers who aren't GST registered
  • private spending, and wages or drawings
  • the private share of mixed-use costs. If your phone is 50% business, include only half the bill.

Box 12: Box 11 × 3 ÷ 23. IRD's guide (IR375) says to calculate it this way, rather than adding up the GST on each receipt.

Box 13, credit adjustments: usually zero.

Box 14: Box 12 plus Box 13.

Box 15: pay or refund?

Box 15 is the difference between Box 10 and Box 14. If Box 10 is bigger, you have GST to pay. If Box 14 is bigger, you get a refund. If they're equal, it's a nil return, but you still have to file it.

Worked example

Sam is a designer who files two-monthly. In April–May 2026 Sam received $1,725 and $3,450 from NZ clients and $2,400 from a US client (zero-rated). Sam's GST-claimable spending came to $2,546.29: software, half a phone bill, the business share of fuel and power, and a laptop.

When is it due?

A GST return is due on the 28th of the month after the period ends, with two exceptions. Periods ending 30 November are due on 15 January, and periods ending 31 March are due on 7 May. If the due date falls on a weekend or public holiday, it moves to the next working day.

Common mistakes

  • Claiming GST on bank fees or insurance excess payments that had no GST.
  • Putting GST-exclusive amounts in Box 5 or Box 11. Both boxes are GST-inclusive.
  • Claiming the full GST on a car or phone that's partly private.
  • Forgetting zero-rated sales in Box 5. They go in Box 5 and Box 6.

Source: Inland Revenue, GST guide (IR375) and the GST101A return form.
