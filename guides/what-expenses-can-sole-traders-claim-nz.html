What expenses can sole traders claim in NZ? A practical list

The basic rule: you can claim an expense if you incurred it to earn your business income. If it's partly private, you claim only the business part. If it's capital (something that lasts, like equipment over the threshold), it's depreciated over time instead of claimed all at once.

Usually fully deductible

  • Materials and stock you use or sell.
  • Subcontractors you pay to do business work.
  • Software, subscriptions and website costs.
  • Advertising and marketing.
  • Accounting, tax agent and business legal fees.
  • Business insurance, such as public liability or professional indemnity.
  • Bank and payment-processing fees on business accounts.
  • Interest on business loans.
  • ACC levies.
  • Training related to your current business.
  • Protective clothing and uniforms that are specific to the work.
  • Business travel and accommodation.

Partly deductible

Mixed-use costs. Claim only the business share of your phone, internet, vehicle and home. Base the share on actual use: a vehicle logbook, a phone usage estimate, or the floor area of your home office. Keep a note of how you worked it out.

Entertainment. Most business entertainment, like taking a client out for a meal, is 50% deductible. Light refreshments at work, such as morning tea, are 100% deductible. IRD’s guide IR268 has the full rules. If you're GST registered, you make a once-a-year GST adjustment on the non-deductible half.

Assets: the $1,000 rule

If a business asset (a laptop, tool or piece of equipment) costs $1,000 or less, you can claim the full cost in the year you buy it. If it costs more, you claim it gradually through depreciation. This $1,000 threshold applies to assets bought from 17 March 2021.

If you're GST registered, you can still claim the GST on an expensive asset straight away. Only the income tax deduction is spread out.

Not deductible

  • Private and household spending, such as groceries, personal clothing or the private share of mixed-use costs.
  • Your own "wages" or drawings. Paying yourself isn't an expense for a sole trader.
  • Income tax and GST paid to IRD.
  • Fines and penalties.
  • Loan principal repayments. Only the interest is deductible.

Keep the evidence

Keep a receipt or invoice for every expense you claim (a photo is fine) and keep your records for 7 years. If you're GST registered, you also need taxable supply information (an invoice with the right details) to claim the GST.

Sources: Inland Revenue IR3B schedule notes (low-value assets), Entertainment expenses guide (IR268), and Smart business guide (IR320).
