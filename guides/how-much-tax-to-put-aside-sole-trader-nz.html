How much tax should a sole trader put aside in NZ? (2026–27)

No one deducts tax from a sole trader's income, so it's your job to put it aside. A common rule of thumb is "save 20–30%", but the right figure depends on your profit, your other income and whether you're GST registered. Here's how to work it out.

Step 1: income tax on your profit

You pay income tax on your profit (income minus deductible expenses), not on everything you earn. The rates for individuals from 1 April 2025 are:

Taxable incomeRate
$0 – $15,60010.5%
$15,601 – $53,50017.5%
$53,501 – $78,10030%
$78,101 – $180,00033%
$180,001 and over39%

Each rate applies only to the income within its band.

Worked examples (no other income)

ProfitIncome taxShare of profit
$40,000$1,638 + $4,270 = $5,90814.8%
$70,000$1,638 + $6,632.50 + $4,950 = $13,220.5018.9%
$100,000$1,638 + $6,632.50 + $7,380 + $7,227 = $22,877.5022.9%

If you also have a job

Wages use up the lower tax bands first, so your business profit is taxed at your top rate. If you earn $60,000 in wages, every dollar of side-hustle profit up to $78,100 total is taxed at 30%, and above that at 33%. Set aside more than the averages in the table above.

Step 2: GST, if you're registered

If you charge GST, 3/23 of every GST-inclusive payment (about 13%) isn't yours. Put it aside on top of your income tax saving, minus the GST you can claim back on expenses.

Step 3: ACC

ACC invoices self-employed people separately for earner and work levies, based on your income. These aren't part of income tax, so allow a little extra. ACC levies are tax-deductible.

Step 4: provisional tax

If your residual income tax (the tax left to pay after PAYE and other deductions) is over $5,000, you'll usually pay provisional tax the following year. Under the standard option, that's last year's tax plus 5%, in three instalments due 28 August, 15 January and 7 May. Your first year in business can bring a big bill: last year's tax plus the first instalments of this year. Saving from day one avoids that shock.

A simple routine

  1. Open a separate savings account called "Tax".
  2. Each time a client pays, move your set-aside % (plus the GST part, if registered) into it.
  3. Recalculate your % every couple of months as your profit becomes clearer.

Source: Inland Revenue, tax rates for individuals and provisional tax pages, ird.govt.nz.
