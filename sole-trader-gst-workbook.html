What's inside

  • Income log: GST split automatically. Mark exported or zero-rated work with one click.
  • Expense log: 24 categories with sensible GST defaults. Bank fees, interest and ACC levies are GST-free, and private costs are excluded.
  • Mixed-use costs: your home office and vehicle % are applied for you, or you can override the % on any line (for example, a 50% business phone).
  • Assets over $1,000: the GST is claimed, but the cost is flagged for depreciation, not expensed.
  • GST Return tab: GST101A boxes 5–15 for monthly, two-monthly or six-monthly filing, with due dates (including the 15 January and 7 May exceptions).
  • Profit & Tax tab: expense totals by category, net profit, income tax on the 2025 brackets (counting your wages), residual income tax, and a provisional tax check for next year.
  • Monthly tab and Key Dates tab.
  • A filled-in example file, so you can see how everything works before you start.

Who it's for

Sole traders earning roughly $10k–$150k, GST registered or not, who file their own IR3 (or hand tidy figures to an accountant).

Who it's not for

Companies, partnerships, employers running payroll, and anyone who wants bank feeds or someone else to file for them.

Honest limits

Uses the GST payments basis. Doesn't calculate ACC earner levies, student loan repayments, tax credits or depreciation. It's a record-keeping tool, not tax advice. Every rate and date is on the Settings tab, so you can see exactly what it assumes.

How delivery works

Pay through Payhip's secure checkout (card or PayPal). You get a download link straight away, on screen and by email. To use it in Google Sheets, upload the .xlsx file to Google Drive and open it with Google Sheets.
